## 4. The big lesson of the session

Radio's "new stem starts a second late" took **five** attempts. Four were correct fixes to
genuinely cold things that changed nothing: the rubberband render, the stretch-duration IPC round
trip, the engine's decoded buffer (a new `preload-stem` message), and the waveform peaks.

The actual cause was an **ordering bug** (`705510a`): `commitSlotPick` and `armRadioPick` ran in
one microtask, and `pickForSlot` fires its IPC *before any await*, while the commit's engine push
goes through `requestAnimationFrame` — a frame later. **The next pick's 0.3–3.1s of main-process
work was in flight before the current change's `load-project` was even sent.**

**The rule: measure before warming anything.** Every one of those four fixes was defensible from
the code. A single pasted log from Elling found the real cause in minutes. When something is
slow, instrument the chain and get per-step numbers — do not reason from where the cost *looks*
like it should be.

A corollary, learned twice more the same day: an agent told to "warm cache X" checked first and
found X was **not on the path at all** (`pitchCache`/`bandEnergyCache` — Discover renders
`<Waveform>` without `showPitchLine` and never renders `PolarGlyph`). Warming them would have
added load to a renderer already dying. **Verify a hypothesis is even reachable before building
on it.**

## 5. Environment facts that dominate performance here

Invisible from inside the repo, and they explain most of what looks like slow code:

- **His Endlesss archive is on a USB/ExFAT volume.** macOS does not hold its pages, so a "warm"
  read is reliably cold: `listJams` measured **1389ms cold vs 41ms warm**; `existsSync` is
  **220µs** a call; a cold `readdirSync` is **626ms** and no yield policy can subdivide a syscall
  (**still unfixed** — needs an async listing).
- **Count-based yields are wrong on that volume.** `listLibraryScanTargets` yielded every 200
  rows = ~44ms of uninterruptible blocking between yields, which every main-process consumer
  queued behind. Fixed to an 8ms budget (`5b0a91b`): max slice 102ms → 9.8ms. **Other count-based
  yields remain** in `discoverCandidates.ts` (`CLASSIFY_YIELD_EVERY`, `RIFF_QUERY_YIELD_EVERY`)
  and several caches.
- **The renderer OOM'd at ~4GB** after ~7 minutes of radio, with ~968ms mark-compact pauses.
  `DiscoverLibraryScan` decodes 3 stems/500ms at 5–15MB live each, always on. Radio now holds it
  (`c5612da`), but **this does not fix OOM outside radio.** The suspected retention (a
  never-closed `OfflineAudioContext` in `resampleTo16kMono.ts`, a per-request `samples.slice()`
  in `stemAnalysisClient.ts`) is **theory** — it needs a heap snapshot, which no agent here can
  take.
- **His home wifi drops unicast between clients.** The phone remote cannot work over that LAN;
  see `[[phone-lan-client-isolation]]` in memory for the one-minute tcpdump proof and the six
  wrong turns not to repeat.

## 7. Known bugs and gaps, none urgent

- **`maskKindIndexCache` rebuilds 435,740 rows on a ~400-row delta.** The invalidation is
  *correct* (it really does read `StemAutoCategory`); it is an incremental-index problem. Radio's
  scan hold gets most of the payoff for free. Do **not** "fix the signal" — that would serve stale
  roles.
- **Own-library riffs have no authorship.** `listRiffsInJam` (`endlesssApi.ts:883`) hardcodes
  `userName: ''` because the CouchDB jam-listing view has no per-riff author. So the new jam
  filter works on a LORE archive and degrades honestly on the synced library. `resolveJamRiff`
  probably has the author and nobody threads it through. This also blocks a "more from this
  person" radio nudge.
- **The mac update feed** was fixed (`3520215`) but **never exercised by a real release** — the
  guard fails the release if the feed does not describe both architectures. Watch it on 1.3.1.
- **A stale v1.2.0 draft release** still exists on GitHub, superseded by 1.3.0. Elling's to delete.
- **`phraseCache` has no eviction function**, unlike its three siblings.
- **Two-stem crossfades are deferred** — not an engine limit, three renderer 1:1 assumptions, one
  of which would break the phone mixer.
